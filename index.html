A

What you feed it

Four columns per ad, all exportable from Ads Manager at ad level — plus one target.

The necessary inputs

  • Amount spent — standard Ads Manager column, ad level.
  • New customers — the count of first-time buyers the ad produced. This is the one that decides everything (see the warning below).
  • Reach — unique people reached, standard Ads Manager column. The tool works out CPMr = spend ÷ reach × 1,000 itself.
  • Days running (optional) — only used for the reach-play grace period in the verdicts.
  • Target new-customer CPA — one account-level number, set at the top of the page and on Chart 1. New-customer CPA itself is also worked out for you: spend ÷ new customers.
⚠ The one gotcha: Ads Manager's default “Conversions / Results” counts all purchases, including repeat buyers — that flatters CPA and will put ads in the scale zone that don't belong there. Use a new-customer conversion instead: a first-purchase custom conversion in Events Manager, or new buyers matched back from your store/CRM. And pull every ad over the same date range and attribution window, or the comparisons aren't fair.

How the two charts combine

  • Scale + efficient growth → scale more aggressively than the usual 5–15% — cheap reach and a proven CPA.
  • Scale + precision → normal 5–15% raises, watching that CPA holds as spend climbs — the reach is pricey, so there's less slack.
  • Kill + reach play → don't kill yet: bump the target CPA 10–20% for that ad first. Cheap reach often starts journeys that close later. If it still hasn't closed the gap after ~2 weeks of real spend, get conservative.
  • Kill + kill candidate → cut quickly. Expensive reach and a CPA you're confident misses target is the worst of both charts.
  • Anything in test → keep spending at the current budget and re-check as the dot moves right and the curves close in on it.

What these charts don't see — worth knowing

  • Attribution lag. The newest ads always look worse than they are, because some purchases haven't been credited yet. Don't kill inside your attribution window.
  • What a customer is worth. Two ads at the same CPA aren't equal if one brings bigger baskets or repeat buyers. Target CPA is only as good as the LTV thinking behind it — revenue per new customer is the natural next input (it turns this into a new-customer ROAS view).
  • Fatigue. Frequency and creative wear-out aren't plotted. A scale-zone ad with climbing frequency may not hold its CPA when you raise budget — that's why precision ads scale at the normal pace, not the aggressive one.
  • Zero-customer ads. An ad with spend but no new customers yet has no CPA to plot. It's drawn pinned to the top of Chart 1, and it only becomes a confident kill once spend passes roughly 3× your target with still nothing to show.

How the curves work: for each ad, the spread of possible true CPAs is estimated from its conversion count (a 90% interval — with few customers the truth could be far from the CPA you see; with many, it's pinned down). An ad is a kill when even the optimistic end of that interval misses target, a scale when even the pessimistic end beats it, and test while target still sits inside the interval. The red and green curves are exactly those two boundaries drawn across every spend level, which is why they pinch toward target as spend rises. The kill zone should ideally stay empty in a well-run account — an ad sitting there is spend you could have stopped earlier.
